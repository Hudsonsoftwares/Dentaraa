1. Purpose and scope

This document describes how to build a cloud (SaaS) dental clinic management product

on Odoo 20 Community. It merges the two source documents:

Feature spec (29 modules): rich clinical depth – odontogram, per-tooth history,
implants, crowns, orthodontics, consent, insurance, sterilization, patient portal,

feedback.

Workflow Plan Rev 3 (6 phases): the delivery plan – onboarding, branches,

appointments, consultation, billing, pharmacy, lab, reports, notification wallet, GST, HR

and payroll via Hudson modules.

The goal is one design: what we reuse from Odoo, what we build, how data is structured,

who can do what, and in which phase each item ships. Section 5 is the key table: standard

Odoo vs custom build.

2. Design principles

1. Reuse before build. Contacts, calendar, accounting, inventory, purchase, mail, portal

and HR come from Odoo. We build only dental-specific screens and rules.

2. One record per patient, one per staff member. Patient = a partner extended with
dental data; staff = employee linked to user. No double entry between phases.

3. Tooth-centric clinical model. Every diagnosis, treatment, image and follow-up can link

to a tooth. This powers the per-tooth history, which the spec calls the major
differentiator.

4. Configurable, not hard-coded. Treatments, drop-down lists, templates, message rules,

numbering system and permissions are settings.

5. Mobile first. All screens work on phone and tablet; the odontogram is a standalone

web component.

6. Phase-wise release. Each phase works on its own; modules have clean dependencies.

7. Multi-branch and multi-tenant ready. Branch-level prices, letterhead, tax number,

stock and figures.

3. Assumptions and Odoo 20 Community constraints

Odoo 20 is expected around October 2026. Before each phase, confirm the stable
branch and the behaviour of every reused module. Hudson HRMS modules were built
for Odoo 19 and need porting and testing.

Community has no Studio, no Enterprise Appointments, Sign, Documents, Helpdesk or
Payroll apps. We therefore build: our own booking logic, signature capture (consent
and estimates), document attachments, and use Hudson modules for payroll.

Community includes: Contacts, Calendar, Sales, Purchase, Inventory,
Invoicing/Accounting basics, Website, Portal, Discuss/Mail, Employees, Time Off,

Attendance, Point of Sale (optional). Confirm exact feature set (for example

reconciliation, reports) on Odoo 20.

Messaging (WhatsApp, SMS) needs an external provider and template approval; start

this in Phase 1.

Markets: India first, Gulf possible. This decides GST/VAT setup, languages, RTL, payroll

rules and hosting region.

7. Key data model

7.1 Patient

dental.patient (extends res.partner): patient_id (auto), photo, dob, age (computed),
gender, blood_group, phone, whatsapp, email, address, emergency contact, guardian

(partner), preferred language, marketing source, consent date, active branch.

dental.medical.history: allergies, existing conditions, medications, previous surgeries,

notes.

dental.dental.history: previous treatment, last visit, oral hygiene, smoking, tobacco,
brushing frequency.

Dental Clinic Management on Odoo 20 – Design Document

dental.alert: alert type and text, severity; shown as banner on every clinical and billing

screen.

Several patients may share one phone number; search matches on the first digits.

7.2 Appointment and queue

dental.appointment: number, patient, doctor, date, start, end, type, reason/complaint,
priority, notes, status (pending, confirmed, checked-in, in consultation, completed, no-
show, cancelled).

dental.checkin: appointment or walk-in, token number, check-in time, queue priority,
emergency flag, waiting time (computed), called time.

dental.waitlist: patient, preferred doctor/time window, status.

7.3 Clinical

dental.case: case sheet per patient and doctor; chief complaint, history, examination;
several treatment plans per case.

dental.exam: general (oral hygiene, gingiva, tongue, palate, mucosa, TMJ) and clinical
(tooth condition, mobility, caries, sensitivity, bleeding, probing depth, fracture) plus
rich-text notes.

dental.tooth.state: patient, tooth (stored in FDI), surface set, status (healthy, caries,

filled, missing, extracted, RCT, crown, implant, fractured, impacted), updated by event.

dental.tooth.event (tooth history ledger): patient, tooth, event type, source record
(diagnosis, treatment session, image, lab, implant, follow-up), date, doctor. This one

ledger gives the 36 example: diagnosis → treatment → X-ray → RCT → crown → follow-
up.

dental.diagnosis.master: name, code, description, category, suggested treatments.
dental.diagnosis: patient, tooth, diagnosis, severity, date, doctor, notes.

dental.plan: plan number, patient, doctor, state; dental.plan.line: diagnosis, treatment,
tooth/surface/scope (single, several, full mouth, arch, side), quantity, price, discount,
tax, total, expected duration, visits required, visit counter, status (planned, in progress,

completed), billed flag.

dental.session: treatment, tooth, doctor, date, procedure, materials used, notes,
duration, next visit.

dental.image: patient, tooth, type (IOPA, OPG, CBCT, cephalometric, intraoral,
extraoral, before/after), date, doctor, notes, attachment.

dental.consent.template and dental.consent: type (extraction, RCT, implant, surgery,

anesthesia, photography, orthodontic), signature, date, PDF.

dental.ortho.case and dental.ortho.visit: case number, orthodontist, appliance, start,
expected completion; per visit wire, brackets, elastics, retainer, next date.

dental.rct.canal: working length, file size, taper per canal.

7.4 Prescription, lab, implant

dental.prescription and lines: medicine (product, stock batch optional), dosage,
frequency, duration, route, before/after food, instructions, custom item. Allergy check

against dental.alert at save.

dental.lab.order: number, patient, doctor, tooth, treatment, lab (vendor), material,
shade, due date, cost, status; crown fields: type, margin, abutment, pontic, delivery

date. Materials: zirconia, e-max, PFM, ceramic, metal.

dental.implant: brand, model, serial, diameter, length, tooth, placement date, bone
graft, abutment, crown, healing period, follow-up.

7.5 Money

Invoice lines created from completed plan lines, medicines and other charges
(account.move extended with patient, doctor, plan link).

dental.payment.plan: total, milestones (initial, procedure, final), paid, due, overdue,
next date. Example: ₹60,000 = ₹20,000 + ₹20,000 + ₹20,000.

dental.insurance.policy and dental.claim: provider, policy number, coverage, validity,
claim amount, approved amount, patient payable, status.

Day-end closing: expected vs counted cash, difference, reason.

7.6 Stock and sterilization

Products with lots and expiry (batch number, manufacturing date, expiry, supplier,

quantity); purchase and selling price.

Automations: expiry within 30 days, below minimum stock.

dental.sterilization.cycle: cycle ID, instrument/set, autoclave, operator, start, end,

temperature, pressure, result.

7.7 Recall, follow-up, feedback, wallet

dental.recall.rule: treatment, interval (cleaning 6 months, RCT 7 days, implant 2 weeks,
braces 4 weeks). On plan line completion the system creates a recall activity.

dental.followup: date, treatment, doctor, reason, status, notes; reminder before due
date.

dental.feedback: overall rating, doctor, staff, waiting time, cleanliness, comments.

dental.wallet and dental.wallet.txn: balance, top-ups, per-message debits, message
log.

8. Business rules

1. A doctor cannot have two overlapping appointments or be double-booked; slots

outside working hours or on approved leave are blocked.

2. A patient ID is generated automatically and never reused. Duplicate warnings appear

on phone, name plus DOB match.

3. Assistants can create but not delete clinical records, see no financial totals, and

cannot edit medical notes. Reception sees a read-only summary.

4. Medical alerts show on every consultation, prescription and billing screen. Prescribing

a medicine that clashes with an allergy raises a blocking warning that needs a doctor
override with reason.

5. Completed plan lines move to billing; stage-wise billing is allowed for multi-visit

treatments.

6. Discounts above a threshold require owner approval. Refunds create credit notes.

7. Completing a treatment with a recall rule creates a recall activity automatically; the

recall list is reviewed by reception and marked called with a result.

8. Consent must exist for configured treatments (extraction, RCT, implant, surgery,

anesthesia) before the session can be marked completed (configurable).

9. Prescriptions dispensed from clinic stock reduce stock by batch, first-expiry-first-out.

10. Every tooth event is immutable; corrections add a new event. All clinical edits are

logged in the activity record.

11. Prices are resolved by branch and date. Tax and letterhead follow the branch.

12. Messages deduct from the notification wallet; sending stops when balance is zero and

the owner is alerted at a low balance.

9. Key workflows

Appointment to payment. Book (search phone, choose doctor, time) → pending →

confirmed (message sent) → patient arrives → check-in gives token → waiting queue
(emergency first) → doctor starts consultation → case sheet and examination →
odontogram marking and diagnosis → treatment plan and estimate approved → sessions

executed with materials → prescription and advice → invoice from completed lines →
payment or instalment → recall and follow-up created → feedback request.

Lab/crown. Plan line flagged lab required → lab order with shade and material → vendor

assigned → due date alerts → delivery recorded → fitted in a session → tooth event added
→ follow-up.

Implant. Plan → consent → implant record at placement → healing period timer →
abutment and crown lab order → follow-up schedule.

Insurance. Policy captured at registration → estimate shows insured and patient parts →
claim submitted → approved amount recorded → patient payable invoiced.

10. Screens by role

Admin: onboarding, branches, staff and permissions, treatment and price lists,
templates, message rules, discount approvals, expenses, day-end, reports, wallet,

plans.

Doctor: dashboard (own appointments, check-ins), waiting queue, case sheet,
odontogram, tooth history, plan board, imaging, prescription, lab order, own leave,
attendance and payslip.

Reception: patient search and registration, appointment calendar (day, week, doctor-
wise), check-in and token board, recall list, billing and receipts, dues list, cash counter.

Pharmacist / Inventory: stock, batches, expiry alerts, purchase orders, dispensing.

Patient portal: profile, book and view appointments, history, odontogram,
prescriptions, invoices and payments, documents, plan acceptance, lab status.

11. Notifications and integrations

Templates per event: booking confirmation, next-day reminder, recall, follow-up
reminder, payment receipt, advice after visit, lab ready, review request, daily and
monthly report to owner.

Channels: WhatsApp, SMS, email, with opt-in/out per patient, language per patient and

delivery status.

Payment: UPI and card gateways for wallet top-up and optional portal payments.

Accounting: GST invoices per branch (India); VAT variant for the Gulf if confirmed.

Export: patient data export, CSV and PDF reports.

12. Security, privacy and compliance

Role-based groups and record rules by branch and by doctor; field-level restriction for

medical notes and salary.

Two-step login, login history, activity record for clinical and money changes.

Encrypted backups; hosting region chosen by where patient data must live.

Consent date and privacy terms stored per patient; legal review per market.

Minors handled through guardian link and guardian consent.

13. Non-functional requirements

Responsive on phone and tablet; the odontogram supports touch.

Chart loads under 2 seconds for a full-history patient (tooth events indexed by patient
and tooth).

Large images stored as attachments with thumbnails; CBCT files handled as
downloads or external storage.

Automated tests per module; separate modules and pull-request review as in Revision

2.

Multi-tenant provisioning with feature flags per subscription plan.

16. Risks

Odoo 20 maturity and porting Hudson modules: test reused modules early.

Odontogram complexity: build as an isolated web component in early Phase 3 and test
with real dentists.

Scope for two developers: the spec adds many modules; keep extras optional until
management decides.

Provider approvals for WhatsApp and SMS: start in Phase 1.

Community limits (no Sign, Documents, Appointments): custom signature, document
and booking features add effort.

Sensitive data: access rules, backups, hosting region, legal review.
